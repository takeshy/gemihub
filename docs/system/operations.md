Customer records, inquiries, staff accounts and Calendar connection, and email notifications.

Settings saves retain their request ID and submitted settings in browser storage when a response is lost. Always read the saved settings when restoring this state: if that section already matches, clear the completed retry automatically. Otherwise retain the pending inputs, and offer “Discard pending changes and load saved settings.” This action reads the current server settings before clearing browser state and makes no configuration write. If the server is unavailable or publication is unfinished, keep the pending retry state. A cached staff row may therefore differ from the current shop; never treat it as proof that a removed staff member was restored on the server.

# Customers

Bookings and inquiries are grouped by email address. The shop sees visit counts, the last visit and totals, and can keep private notes with up to five attachments per note. Customers never see notes. Customer periods and inquiry date filters use midnight in the shop's time zone. Notification dates use that time zone; paid/refunded amounts use the payment's saved currency.

# Inquiries

Customers send inquiries from the shop page, with optional attachments. Operators can also create cases manually. A single Record a response form switches between calls, incoming messages, internal notes and email replies. Email shows the recipient and Send and save; other types save a record without mailing it. Email and record drafts, including attachments, are retained separately across switches. Email is unavailable until the contact has an address. All forms allow up to five files, 5MB each and 10MB total. Bytes stay in the owner's private Google Drive. Reply emails contain only files selected for that reply; customer receipt emails include files submitted on the public form. Internal record attachments never go out by email. File downloads require a current operator of the owning shop. New attachments are checked by size and SHA-256 before download or email. A same-size content replacement stops attachment mail retries and records an error. Saved legacy attachments without a checksum retain size-only checks. Each customer can send five inquiries per hour from the shop page, and each shop accepts up to 500MB of customer attachments per day; plain inquiries continue after that cap.

The list and kanban initially include recent resolved cases, while hiding cases resolved at least one shop-local calendar month ago (receipt date for legacy records). Setting either receipt-date bound shows older cases within that range. The screen explains how to show older records. Completion stores a timestamp, reopening clears it, and repeated moves preserve it. Kanban explains desktop drag and drop and the mobile/keyboard status selector. Replying and resolving remain independent.

# Traffic and activity history

Open the management dashboard's dedicated Analytics menu to view traffic, article access, booking cohorts and staff sales over 7, 30 or 90 days. Analytics is separate from Customers and article editing, and is never displayed on public customer pages. This includes visitors who have not signed in or registered. PV counts page displays, including reloads. Daily unique browsers count the same browser once per shop-local calendar day across all pages; article unique browsers count each browser once per article in the chosen period. Dates follow the shop's configured time zone.

The day/month selector groups traffic, cohorts and staff sales trends by local date or YYYY-MM. Monthly totals include only dates within the selected period; the first/current month may be partial. Monthly browser counts deduplicate visitor keys within each month, rather than adding daily unique counts. If the monthly log scan exceeds 20,000 page views, monthly unique counts are unavailable while PV remains exact. Cohort conversion sums completions and entrants before calculating percentages, and a monthly observation window is mature only when every daily cohort in it has matured. Sales add recorded monetary amounts with currency precision. Tracking retention remains 90 days; monthly grouping does not recover older records. Use `get_monthly_traffic` (`logs:monthly-traffic`) for monthly access questions.

Unique browsers approximate people. Separate devices, private browsing, cleared storage and the 90-day browser-ID rotation may count separately. When browser storage is unavailable, a display contributes PV only. Never add daily or article unique counts and call the result overall unique people. Signed-in owners and accepted staff, previews, provisional pages and offline displays are excluded. Views before logging began or that could not be sent are unavailable. These are major page displays rather than complete click tracking. Under extreme traffic each shop counts at most 1,200 views per minute, so totals can be lower than actual views.

Customer details also show signed-in activity history: login, opening booking and My page screens, booking confirmation and cancellation. Booking analytics can connect a browser's anonymous booking-page visit with later signed-in activity on the same browser, using per-shop hashed browser/customer keys. Raw browser IDs and email addresses are not stored in telemetry. Anonymous visits that never acquire an authenticated link cannot be matched to booking completion.

The report selector also offers Booking cohorts and Staff sales and booking conversion. Cohorts group each browser by its first booking-page visit within the selected 7/30/90-day window in the shop time zone. Without browser storage, signed-in customer keys are used instead; unidentified views are reported separately. A server-confirmed standard booking after entry converts that browser once. Across identified devices, the latest entry receives the outcome; transfer purchases and bookings created before entry are excluded. Selection stages are observed reach, not a strictly ordered funnel: defaults, historical data and unsent events can skip a stage. One-day/seven-day columns use 24/168 hours after entry and show Observing until the entire daily cohort has matured. Newer cohorts have shorter observation windows. All telemetry is retained for 90 days. When a relevant log kind exceeds the 20,000-event scan limit, conversion is unavailable; select a shorter period. Never use partial counts as full conversion rates.

Staff sales sum recorded prices of active appointments whose service has ended, grouped by the service end date in the shop time zone. They exclude cancelled, no-show and future appointments and differ from payment receipts. Daily sales trends can show all staff or one staff member. Staff selection/assignment counts each observed browser once per staff member; multiple choices count for each staff member, while confirmation belongs to the final assigned staff. No-preference confirmations count for the assigned staff as well. Staff conversion is based on entry cohorts, whereas sales use service end dates.

For actual counts, assistant research uses `get_daily_traffic` (`logs:traffic`), `get_article_access_counts` (`logs:articles`), and `get_booking_analytics` (`logs:booking-analytics`), shared with the management reports. Use `search_shop_logs` (`logs:shop`) for access, errors, PayPal processing diagnostics and signed-in activity. Generic log search is bounded; do not use its truncated counts instead of traffic aggregates. Answers can include Mermaid charts rendered locally with Pele, with text, units, source references and a source-data fallback. Only owners and accepted staff can read shop reports and histories.

# Staff

Analytics also offers Menu sales: base services and add-ons are listed separately with appointment counts and daily/monthly sales trends. These totals use the same active, ended appointments and end-date window as staff sales. For bookings with saved items, the saved discounted booking total is apportioned by saved item prices, using integer minor units and largest remainders so the sum matches exactly. Current catalog prices are never used to reconstruct historical allocations. Multi-menu bookings without a saved price breakdown appear in Unknown breakdown. A menu's count is the number of appointments containing it, so the counts of different menus can overlap. The shared `get_booking_analytics` tool returns these figures in `menus`; menu trends include only dates with activity.

Booking charts use plain labels about booking page visits and confirmations rather than cohort jargon. Multiple-series charts have color-matched legends within the chart card; the plot keeps its full width on phones. Series are never described by their order. Mermaid source code is hidden in ordinary use and is available only when a diagram cannot be rendered. The numerical report tables remain visible.

Staff/menu sales trends show all staff or menus simultaneously as separate named lines with aligned daily/monthly dates. Staff sales use one comparison trend chart, alongside the totals and detail table; no separate staff-ranking bar chart is shown. Selecting one preserves its comparison color. Menu sales also show a pie chart for the selected period's overall composition, with amounts and percentages in the legend. Zero sales do not form slices; nonzero shares below one percent are retained, and shares below 0.1% are labeled accordingly. Duplicate names remain separate by menu ID. The numeric pie uses React SVG shapes so small shares and the full-circle case are represented accurately.

Selecting one staff member changes that same single chart to a menu-by-menu comparison for that staff member. The totals and detail table also use only their appointments. The report returns each staff row's `menus`, built from that staff member's ended active appointments using the same saved-price allocation as overall menu sales. Individual staff selection persists when changing day/month grouping, the period, or refreshing. Menu colors match the overall menu report. With no ended appointments, the report shows an empty-state explanation and zero trend. No additional staff-specific graphs are added.

The overall Menu sales report uses two charts: composition pie and simultaneous menu trend comparison. No separate top-menu ranking bar chart is displayed. The detail table remains available for exact amounts and counts.

The Menu for sales trend dropdown affects only the trend chart: All menus compares menus, while choosing one menu compares that menu's revenue for each staff member using `staff[].menus`. The overall composition pie, totals and menu detail table do not change with this dropdown.

The owner grants Calendar FreeBusy access during normal owner sign-in, alongside Drive and Gmail permissions. The owner is a booking provider without a staff registration, and their calendar is checked automatically. New shops start with no additional staff. The owner has no separate calendar status panel; ordinary sign-in handles consent. Legacy explicit staff assignments remain supported. Other staff sign in through the staff login link with their own Google account and can manage bookings, inquiries and customer notes, but not shop settings or publication. Busy times in each connected calendar block booking slots for the corresponding staff member.

# Notifications

gemihub sends email from the owner's Gmail for: booking confirmation, payment confirmation, cancellation and refund results, opening alerts, inquiry replies and staff invitations. The shop's `notificationEmail` receives a copy of booking-related mail, except opening alerts, which go to the customer alone. Delivery is at least once; a lost response can cause a duplicate email, never a duplicate booking.

Pending Google integration jobs do not show a global warning banner in management screens, including after a page release. The background queue and automatic retries continue independently of this display. Incomplete publication and payment-specific alerts retain their existing behavior.

Google integration failures are tracked as incidents rather than inferred from retry counts. Jobs record their mail kind, booking or inquiry reference, first-failure time, last-attempt time, terminal reason, and incident ID. An incident aggregates a shop, operation type, safe error class, and occurrence generation under a persistent ID. A transient failure that recovers below the threshold notifies nobody. Mail notifies a developer after five minutes and three failures; Drive article export after fifteen minutes and three failures. Authentication loss, configuration defects, unexpected internal errors, and final failure notify immediately. A 403 is classified by its Google reason: only missing authentication or scope may ask the owner to reconnect, while rate limits are transient and disabled APIs or policy errors are configuration defects. Unknown 403s notify the developer without asking for a reconnect. Stalled jobs and a missing Google credential are detected even though they do not increment failure counts. Obsolete, cancelled, expired, or archived targets end an incident without counting as recovery.

Developer alerts go to an HTTPS webhook configured with `OPS_WEBHOOK_URL`, through a notification outbox separate from mail and Drive jobs. The canonical event is converted by a `generic`, Slack or Google Chat adapter selected with `OPS_WEBHOOK_FORMAT`; an unknown format is not posted blindly. Alerts include only the environment, shop ID, incident ID, operation, safe error class and code, occurrence time, duration, affected count, retry state, and log ID. They never include customer identities, addresses, message bodies, inquiry text, recipients, attachments, credentials, or raw exception text. Retries of the same incident do not create more alerts; an open incident sends a six-hour summary, and an incident that alerted on opening sends one recovery alert. A recurrence after recovery is a new incident generation and sends a new opening alert. A message that can never be sent is its own incident: it alerts once without summaries, closes when a later message of the same kind reaches that inquiry, and otherwise expires quietly after `OPS_FINAL_ALERT_DAYS` (7 by default). Closed incidents are deleted after `OPS_INCIDENT_RETENTION_DAYS` (30 by default). Notification failure never changes a booking, payment, mail, or Drive result, and a stopped channel is detected by separate log-based, queue-age, and uptime monitoring.

Owners see confirmed impact and the next action, not technical errors. A Google reconnect is shown only when authentication or scope loss is confirmed; a 403 caused by rate limits, a disabled API, policy, or an unknown reason uses the impact message instead. Delayed mail appears after 30 minutes or earlier when a booking starts within two hours, a final inquiry-reply failure appears immediately, and a Drive copy delay appears only in the dashboard's Needs attention area. A mail failure alone never claims that a booking or payment failed. The existing publication and PayPal alerts and all retry limits are unchanged.
